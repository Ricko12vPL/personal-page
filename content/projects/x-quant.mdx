> **Correction (October 2026).** Earlier versions of this page presented a frozen momentum strategy (`cfg_00` / MetaEnsemble v56) as CSCV-validated and trading on a paper account, with headline overfitting and out-of-sample metrics. That system was formally **rejected on 2 August 2026 and decommissioned on 9 August 2026**. The metrics previously shown here are withdrawn and should not be cited.

## Overview

X_Quant is my solo quantitative-research program. It has had two lives, and the second one exists because the first one failed review.

**v1** grew over two years into a ≈274K-line trading platform built around a single strategy. An internal post-mortem rejected it. **v2** started the same week, from zero, with the post-mortem turned into a written research constitution — and spent seven weeks measuring, under that constitution, whether any strategy I could afford to trade had a real edge. None did, and every one of those answers is on record.

## v1 (2024–2026): what the post-mortem found

v1 was retired on 2026-08-09. Its post-mortem is not a single verdict but a set of lessons that v2's standards now name explicitly:

- **PBO had been computed in-sample.** v2's rule: *"PBO only on the out-of-sample matrix — PBO on in-sample data is not PBO."*
- **Free-form feature discovery turned it into "an overfitting machine".** v2 allows machine learning only to *combine* at most three pre-registered signals, never to search for features.
- **Results were tuned until they passed**, and paper-fill cost assumptions were never verified.
- **Eleven engineering failure patterns**, each now paired with a guard rule: code and tests reading dead copies of the tree; a monitor that overloaded the very resource it was watching; gates that reported "clean" when they had not measured anything; empty results read as success; tests that wrote to the live journal; multi-hour runs with no checkpoints; evidence kept outside version control — and four more.

The through-line: v1 had validation *machinery*, but not a validation *process* that could stop me from believing my own numbers.

## v2 (August–September 2026): a process built to kill bad ideas cheaply

v2 is governed by a written constitution of eight articles. A result produced in breach of any of them is void, however good it looks. The core of it:

- **Truth of measurement** — causality checked per bar; PBO only out-of-sample; folds that cover 2008, 2011, 2020 and 2022; pre-registration before the run, one shot; data pinned by hashed manifests.
- **The instrument is not the strategy** — the measurement harness lives apart from strategies; *build*, *verify* and *repair* are separate sessions, and no session judges its own work.
- **Limits are law** — every run has a wall-clock budget; gates fail closed; *zero means "not measured", never "success"*.
- **A register of truth** — an append-only decision register; a wrong entry is corrected by a new entry, never by editing the old one.
- **Edge before code** — no strategy without a written, falsifiable hypothesis: who pays, why it isn't arbitraged away, and how its death will be recognised.
- **Honest data from day zero** — total-return prices and point-in-time constituents; survivorship-biased data is not data.
- **One engine** for research and production, with bit-for-bit parity.

Around that sit a **permanent attempts ledger** (every configuration ever evaluated counts toward the deflated Sharpe of every future test — 2,976 attempts by the last recorded run), a **sealed 2018–2025 holdout** that only a pre-registered one-shot confirmation may open, an incident log where an incident closes only when its root cause is removed, and decisions taken in a recorded dialogue with an **adversarial AI partner** (Claude) whose objections stay in the register.

By 23 September, in the register's own words: *"7 weeks and 8 programmes: 0 candidates."* The futures track that followed added none. Nothing ever reached paper or live trading.

### The Carver replication

The deepest line of work replicated a published trend-following system on ETFs, under one frozen specification per version:

- **v1.0** (85 ETFs, six rules) reproduced the author's gross Sharpe — **+1.00** — but was **−1.46 net** at 25k: small orders cannot carry retail costs.
- **v1.1** applied the author's own cost rule: **0 of 528** rule–instrument pairs were admissible at any capital up to 50k.
- **v1.2.1** was fitted to the capital (four ETFs, two rules), calibrated against a martingale first, then run once: **gross −0.07, net −0.14**, at the **45th percentile** of the null distribution — indistinguishable from no signal.

A separate verify session reproduced the verdict from the committed artifacts and found one wrong number in the register itself (positive years: 4, not 3). It was corrected by a new entry, and the standard was tightened so that a number may enter the register only from a numeric artifact, never from report prose. The conclusion that closed the line: the author's edge is **breadth**, and breadth under his cost rule needs at least ~100k on ETFs.

### The NQ futures track

In the last week, a futures track tested a public third-party rule set and the strategies built around it:

- Reproducing the third party's published trade ledger **confirmed its numbers, not an edge** — without its ten best days, it was negative.
- A one-shot out-of-sample test of an ensemble of variants: Sharpe 0.19, profit factor 1.016 — **failed** four of five criteria.
- An ensemble with an in-sample Sharpe of 2.84 fell to **−0.88 and −0.48** on clean out-of-sample windows — **rejected as overfit**.
- A second ensemble passed its out-of-sample test formally (Sharpe 1.18), but its gain came from the window in which its families were discovered — **invalidated** as selection leakage.
- A walk-forward of the *whole selection procedure* over 2014–2026 reached Sharpe 0.36 against 0.8 and PSR 90.2% against 95% — **failed**, with no threshold loosened.
- A pooled gradient-boosting model across seven markets: walk-forward Sharpe −0.42 — **failed**, with no tuning and no second attempt allowed.

A prediction-market track got as far as a design package; no model was built.

## What it produced

The register's closing assessment names the real output: not a strategy, but **the instrument** — an engine, a register, a calibration method and a set of executable gates that can close a research question with evidence in a few sessions, and that refuse to let me fool myself. That instrument is what carried over.

## Status

Dormant since 29 September 2026; the repository is not public. Its successor is [Alpha Lab](/projects/alpha-lab), which reads X_Quant's data read-only and deliberately starts its own attempts ledger from zero.

## Tech Stack

Python, pandas, NumPy, PyArrow / Parquet, LightGBM, pytest with mutation tests on the gates, hashed data manifests, git-pinned pre-registrations, and append-only Markdown registers.
