## Overview

A domain-agnostic, multi-agent research automation system designed to conduct frontier research from ideation through a submission-ready package (submission itself is human-only) — with one structural rule: **nothing self-certifies**. Every role output must pass an executable (machine-checkable) gate plus adversarial sign-off from a paired critic role.

The project began as a dual artifact — a reusable organizational structure (39 agent role files plus the orchestrator, in 13 divisions, 9-phase lifecycle) and an empirical record of what happened when it ran. It has since grown into a three-part research pipeline: the **writing org** (this artifact), a real **research program** it selected and designed (C1, now paused at its pre-registration checkpoint), and a separate **adversarial pre-review org** (System B) that hardens a paper before any human submits it. Released on GitHub (latest tag v0.2.1, a documentation erratum); the v0.2.0 release is archived on Zenodo with DOI [10.5281/zenodo.20645678](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20645678).

## What Happened When It Ran

**v0.1.0 (first end-to-end run):** the organization examined 5 candidate research directions and **abandoned all 5** at the gates. The flagship integrity exhibit was itself **retracted** after failing its own verifier. Zero surviving theses.

That result left a critical question open: were the gates correctly identifying flaws — or was the system just a uniform rejector that kills everything it touches?

**v0.2.0 (the calibration study):** a pre-registered, blinded, seeded study answered it. A corpus of 20 theses — 10 known-flawed (with planted defects across 6 flaw classes) and 10 known-sound reproductions — was evaluated under salted filenames with SHA-256 sealed labels.

## Calibration Results

| Metric | M1-ON (round-1 auto-fail) | M1-OFF (ablation) |
|--------|---------------------------|-------------------|
| Sensitivity (correct-reason attribution) | 13/15 = 0.87 [0.60, 0.98] | 14/15 = 0.93 [0.68, 1.00] |
| Plants missed entirely | 0 of 15 | 0 of 15 |
| Known-flawed theses killed | 10 of 10 | 10 of 10 |
| Validity-gate specificity *(exploratory, L8)* | 0.72 [0.58, 0.84] | 0.96 [0.86, 1.00] |
| Known-sound theses killed *(exploratory, L8)* | 8 of 10 | 1 of 10 |

What stands: rejections cited the correct flaw class at the correct location for **13 of 15** planted flaws with the prior on and **14 of 15** with it off. Attribution is measured within the flawed arm, against sealed per-plant ground truth, so the erratum below leaves it unaffected. Every known-flawed thesis was killed in both arms, so no planted flaw was missed entirely; that is raw rejection, which a uniform rejector or a genre heuristic would also produce, so it is reported as measured rather than as unaffected. The gates are still not a uniformly rejecting filter: even allowing for the genre confound, sound theses were not uniformly killed (pooled specificity 0.72 and 0.96, far above the near-zero a uniform rejector predicts; most clearly with the prior off, where 1 of 10 sound theses was killed).

What is now exploratory: the original headline was about the **round-1 auto-fail prior** ("nothing passes review on the first try"). On this corpus it lowered pooled specificity from 0.96 to 0.72 and killed 8 of 10 known-sound theses, with no observed sensitivity benefit (0.87 vs 0.93; the intervals overlap). **Erratum v0.2.1** (2026-06-13) added limitation L8, a genre confound between the corpus arms: the sound theses were self-identifying reproductions and the flawed theses novel proposals, so a gate could score high specificity partly by recognizing genre without reading content. The pooled specificity, the sound-arm kill-rate contrast and the rationale for flipping the M1 default are therefore downgraded to exploratory. The default stays flipped as a design choice; the release states that it is unvalidated on fresh data and that the planned validation must use matched-pair arms.

## From Artifact to Operator: A Real Research Program (C1)

After the calibration study the organization stopped being a demo and started doing the job it was built for. In a follow-on research program it ran its own selection process — generating a 12-candidate slate, freezing it in a single commit before any judgment (2026-06-11), then killing 10 candidates at four non-compensatory gates (asset, falsifier, feasibility, novelty). One was selected: **C1 — "Evaluator Calibration at Scale"** (C8 also passed the gates and was recorded as runner-up).

C1 turns the v0.2.0 idea outward. Instead of measuring the org's own gates, it measures *the field's*: a cross-model benchmark of whether third-party LLM paper-review systems reject flawed research **for the right reasons**, designed for pre-registration (pre-registration drafted, not yet frozen).

- **Cross-model, cross-family.** Three distinct third-party reasoning-model families: OpenAI (GPT-5), Google (Gemini) and Mistral (EU; provisional pin Mistral Small 4). The roster is selected, not frozen. OpenReviewer (a Meta-Llama fine-tune) was carded but not selected, because its prose output cannot be scored for structured attribution. The author's own model family is barred from every confirmatory cell — it may *seed* flaws, never *judge* them.
- **Matched-pair, single-flaw corpus.** Each flawed document is twinned with a sound version from the same pipeline and genre, differing only in one planted defect, across six flaw classes (mechanical vs epistemic) — the design answer to the genre confound (L8) found in the calibration study.
- **Attribution, not just rejection.** It separates "rejected" from "rejected for the correct reason," measures false-kills on the sound twins, and tests a **same-family seeding effect** — the construction-validity axes that SoundnessBench (arXiv:2605.30329, the predecessor that established the bare deficit) structurally lacks.
- **External human ground truth.** Every plant will be certified by ≥2 independent non-author raters under a frozen rubric (pooled Cohen's κ ≥ 0.70) before the corpus is sealed; no rater has been contracted yet. Raters are to be offered co-authorship *unconditionally*, before any rating begins, so the certification cannot be biased by the result.

The governance is the point. An independent circularity ("F6") review — by a separate reviewer that is itself an LLM of the same model family — imposed 8 binding conditions, and the design carries a deviations log, a budget ledger (caps: $7,000 external, $3,000 API hard stop) and a prior-art "collision watch". When four independent LLM assessments (two from other model families) flagged "no independent baseline detector" as the #1 weakness of the line's own first paper (the calibration-study write-up), a **single-judge baseline arm** was added *before* any confirmatory run — and a "multi-gate wall" description of C1's detector in an earlier design draft was **retracted** as inaccurate (the only real structural multiplicity is cross-model majority voting).

### The Cross-Model Pilot — and What It Found

The API blocker cleared. Real third-party keys were provisioned and a directional power-input pilot ran across three reasoning families — **OpenAI GPT-5, Google Gemini 3.1 Pro, and DeepSeek-R1**, all of which cleared the ≥80% structured-output feasibility filter. Of the other candidates tried for the third slot, OpenReviewer (prose output, not attribution-coercible) and Qwen (degenerate: always PASS) failed the filter; Kimi-K2 engaged correctly but was not selected, because a reasoning model (DeepSeek-R1) was preferred. DeepSeek-R1 was later replaced by Mistral (below). The pilot is explicitly a power input, never a finding — but the spread it surfaced is exactly what the benchmark exists to measure:

| Evaluator (pilot, n=15/arm) | Correct-reason | Wrong-reason | False-kill (sound) |
|-----------------------------|----------------|--------------|--------------------|
| Gemini 3.1 Pro (fixed adapter) | **0.93** | 0.07 | 0.67 |
| GPT-5 | 0.47 | 0.20 | 0.40 |
| DeepSeek-R1 | 0.47 | 0.13 | 0.13 |

All rows are directional power inputs at n = 15. The Gemini row is the fixed-adapter run on the full set; an earlier run lost some Gemini outputs to truncation.

The pilot also changed the primary hypothesis. H1 ("systems reject flawed work for the *wrong* reason") needs ≥2 of 3 evaluators above a 0.15 wrong-reason rate. The first two systems measured straddled that threshold (GPT-5 above it, Gemini below), which put H1 at **kill risk**. So on 2026-06-23, before DeepSeek-R1 was measured and before any confirmatory data, the primary was re-selected to **H1″**: the per-system, per-flaw-class *calibration card*, with a pre-specified mechanical-vs-epistemic axis as its confirmatory test. It was chosen as the framing least exposed to a kill given the straddle; the earlier same-family pilot's flaw-class ordering ran against that axis's predicted direction, and that is disclosed. H1 was not falsified: it is retained as exploratory, and with all three families measured the ≥2-of-3 test was indeterminate at n = 15. Confirmatory sample size was ratified at **~300 per arm per system** (±0.057–0.06). The author's own timed adjudication packet clocked a median of ~12 min/item at 32/32 correct verdicts (an upper bound), selecting the Variant-B design.

### The Governance Halting the Author — Again

The pre-registration lock was attempted on 2026-07-01 — and **halted itself at the roster-eligibility gate**: the DeepSeek API terms had not yet been cached and checked by the program's license-review gate (an agent role), and under the program's own rule an unresolved license makes a system ineligible. Rather than freeze anyway, the third evaluator slot was reassigned to **Mistral** on roster-stability and data-governance grounds, not capability. The program's stated rationale is that an open-weight (Apache 2.0), EU model can be self-hosted if hosted access is ever restricted, so the confirmatory run cannot become unexecutable mid-study. The change is logged as a pre-freeze deviation and was driven back through the repair cycles.

### Where C1 Stands

As of the last logged entry (2026-07-05), C1 is **dormant at its pre-registration checkpoint**, pending one author action (an API key for the Mistral feasibility check); nothing has been logged since. After that come the eligibility gate, a deliberate lock and pre-registration freeze, and a separate approval of the confirmatory spend. External spend so far is **about $6, all on directional pilots** ($5.81 OpenAI/Gemini, ~$0.06 router, $0.21 DeepSeek); **$0 of the confirmatory budget** has been spent. Nothing is frozen and no confirmatory tag has been applied.

## System B: Adversarial Pre-Review

A second, separate organization now sits between writing and submission. Where the research org *produces* work, **System B** tries to *destroy* it — a simulated hostile reviewer (7 agents: a rubric-internalizer, five specialist reviewers, and an area chair) that reviews a paper against the **real, frozen rubric of its target venue** before any human submits.

It deliberately **inverts the M1 lesson**: not "reject everything," but high *recall* of flaws with a comfortable accept bar — because the cost asymmetry is reversed (a false accept costs months to a desk-reject; a false reject costs a day of revision). Two rules are non-negotiable: System B can never emit a "submit" — the human gate is non-removable — and because it shares the author's model family, its accept is **necessary but not sufficient**: the external venue is the only terminal authority. Every verdict logs a prediction of the real outcome, so the pipeline calibrates itself against actual peer review over time. Self-test: 13/13 injected flaws caught, 3/3 sound control papers accepted. The calibration-study paper has passed System B's internal pre-review against the REALM @ EMNLP 2026 workshop rubric (round 1 minor revision, round 2 ACCEPT). That is an internal, same-family verdict; no external review outcome is recorded.

## Engineering Highlights

- **Citation Fidelity Protocol (CFP)** — every citation carries a typed claim card with scope conditions and a SHA-256-pinned verbatim anchor; a dedicated `citation-object-adversary` role verifies each card. Built after 3 of 5 v0.1.0 abandonment citations were found misread.
- **Public erratum (v0.2.1, 2026-06-13)** — an independent F6 second review found the L8 genre confound between the calibration corpus arms (sound theses were self-identifying reproductions, flawed theses novel proposals). Pooled specificity (0.96/0.72), the sound-arm kill-rate contrast and the M1-flip rationale were downgraded to exploratory *on the public repo* before any new freeze; within-arm attribution (13/15, 14/15) is unaffected.
- **Executable calibration gates** — `make calibration-analyze` recomputes the calibration numbers from the frozen verdicts and `make verify-citations` checks every citation card. `make reproduce` reruns the retracted v0.1.0 simulation and is expected to fail its integrity check, which confirms the retraction.
- **Pre-registration discipline** — predictions frozen at git tags before any evaluation ran; deviations logged pre-factually, never retrofitted.
- **Limitations-forward reporting** — eight named limitations (L1–L8) stated in full, including small-n, same-model-family evaluation, the spent corpus, and the genre confound added by the erratum.
