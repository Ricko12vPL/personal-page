## Overview

The artifact is a complete research organization expressed as 39 Claude agent roles (one role file each, coordinated by an orchestrator that runs as the main session) across 13 divisions — leadership, frontier research, theory and statistics, interpretability, research engineering, evaluation and red team, safety and governance, literature, scientific writing, visualization, program management, publication, and a self-improvement loop — operating over a 9-phase lifecycle with 4 human checkpoints.

The structural rule that defines the system: **nothing self-certifies**. Every role output passes an executable gate (tests, `make verify`, citation checks, grep gates) plus adversarial sign-off from a paired critic role holding kill authority.

## The Two Empirical Records

**Record 1 — the v0.1.0 run.** The organization examined five candidate research directions and abandoned all five at the gates. Its flagship integrity exhibit was retracted after failing its own verifier. Zero theses survived. This is reported as a finding, not buried: a single run (n = 1) consistent with either working quality control or uniform rejection.

**Record 2 — the calibration study.** A pre-registered (git tag `calibration-prereg-v1`), blinded, seeded study distinguished the two hypotheses. Twenty theses — ten with planted defects across six flaw classes, ten known-sound reproductions — were evaluated under salted filenames with SHA-256 sealed labels, in two arms: M1-ON (round-1 auto-fail prior active) and M1-OFF (ablation).

## Headline Results

| Metric | M1-ON | M1-OFF (ablation) |
|--------|-------|-------------------|
| Sensitivity (correct-reason attribution) | 13/15 = 0.87 [0.60, 0.98] | 14/15 = 0.93 [0.68, 1.00] |
| Plants missed entirely | 0 of 15 | 0 of 15 |
| Known-flawed theses killed | 10 of 10 | 10 of 10 |
| Pooled validity-gate specificity *(exploratory, L8)* | 36/50 = 0.72 [0.58, 0.84] | 48/50 = 0.96 [0.86, 1.00] |
| Known-sound theses killed *(exploratory, L8)* | 8 of 10 | 1 of 10 |

Intervals are 95% exact Clopper–Pearson. What stands: attribution is a within-arm measurement against sealed per-plant ground truth — the correct flaw class at the correct location for 13 of 15 plants (M1-ON) and 14 of 15 (M1-OFF), with no plant missed entirely in either arm — and the gates are not a uniformly rejecting filter. The rows marked exploratory compare the two corpus arms and were downgraded by Erratum v0.2.1 (below). As originally reported, the round-1 auto-fail prior — "nothing passes review on the first try," a plausible-sounding skeptical default — lowered pooled specificity from 0.96 to 0.72 and killed 8 of 10 known-sound theses, with no observed sensitivity benefit (0.87 vs 0.93; the intervals overlap). The default was flipped to off in v0.2.0 and stays off as a design choice; its rationale is now exploratory, and the new default is unvalidated on fresh data (the calibration corpus is spent).

## Erratum v0.2.1 (June 13, 2026)

The erratum is documentation-only: no code, configuration or calibration data changed. An independent F6 second review on 2026-06-12, carried out while the successor program (C1) was selecting its problem, found a genre confound between the corpus arms, now limitation L8. The sound theses were self-identifying reproductions of canonical results and the flawed theses novel proposals, so a gate that passed familiar reproductions and scrutinized novel proposals would score high specificity without reading content. Pooled specificity (0.96/0.72), the sound-arm kill-rate contrast and the M1 default-flip rationale are therefore exploratory; a fresh-corpus validation must use matched-pair arms. Attribution accuracy (13/15, 14/15) is measured within the flawed arm and is unaffected.

## Citation Fidelity Protocol

Three of the five v0.1.0 abandonment citations were found misread on audit (scope overclaim, a smoothing bandwidth read as a block length, a risk metric read as a validation protocol). The v0.2.0 fix is structural: every citation carries a typed claim card — claimed object type, scope conditions, and a verbatim anchor pinned by SHA-256 to its source — verified mechanically and by a dedicated `citation-object-adversary` role.

## Limitations

Eight limitations are stated in full in the release (L1–L8; L8 added in Erratum v0.2.1), including: single run + small-n calibration (wide intervals reported in full), seeder and evaluator drawn from the same model family, named weak points (two flaw classes each detected at 1 of 2 under M1-ON), single-model dependence, the spent corpus behind the M1 default flip, historical citation defects, the retracted exhibit shipped provenance-only, and the genre confound between the corpus arms (L8).

## Reproducibility

The calibration numbers are recomputed from the frozen verdicts with `make calibration-analyze`; `make verify-citations` checks every citation card and `make calibration-prereg-check` verifies that the pre-registration's freeze conditions still hold. `make reproduce` reruns the retracted v0.1.0 simulation and is expected to fail its integrity check, which confirms the retraction rather than reproducing a result. The calibration corpus, all 40 verdicts, sealed-label hashes, pre-registration, and full provenance trail for every abandonment ship in the release. Code is MIT; documentation is CC BY 4.0.

## What Came Next (June–July 2026)

The v0.2.0 artifact became the foundation for a follow-on research program. Three developments followed the release:

**The organization selected and designed a real research project (C1).** Rather than studying its own gates, the org selected — through its own 12-candidate, four-gate selection process — a frontier project that turns the calibration idea outward: a pre-registered, cross-model benchmark of whether *third-party* LLM paper-review systems reject flawed research **for the right reasons**. C1 is designed around a matched-pair, single-flaw corpus whose plants ≥2 independent non-author raters will certify (none contracted yet), three distinct evaluator families (the author's own model family is barred from every confirmatory cell), attribution scoring and a same-family seeding axis — the construction-validity controls that SoundnessBench (arXiv:2605.30329), the predecessor that established the bare deficit, structurally lacks. A directional cross-model pilot (n = 15) measured correct-reason attribution of 0.93 for Gemini 3.1 Pro and 0.47 for both GPT-5 and DeepSeek-R1. GPT-5 and Gemini straddled H1's 0.15 wrong-reason threshold, which put H1 at kill risk, so the per-system *calibration card* (H1″) was chosen as primary on 2026-06-23, before any confirmatory data; H1 is retained as exploratory, and with three families measured its ≥2-of-3 test was indeterminate at n = 15. Confirmatory n was ratified at ~300 per arm per system. C1 is **paused at its pre-registration checkpoint**. A lock attempt on 2026-07-01 halted at the eligibility gate because the DeepSeek API terms had not been cleared by ip-legal review, and the third slot was then reassigned to Mistral on roster-stability and data-governance grounds; since 2026-07-05 the program has been waiting on one API key for the Mistral feasibility check. The selected roster (OpenAI GPT-5, Google Gemini, Mistral) is not frozen. External spend so far is about $6, all on directional pilots; **$0 of the confirmatory budget has been spent, and nothing is frozen.**

**A public erratum (v0.2.1).** The L8 genre confound, found by an independent F6 second review during C1's problem selection, led to the cross-arm specificity results being downgraded to exploratory *on the public repository* before any new freeze (see above) — so the public record matches what the program privately knows.

**A second adversarial pre-review organization (System B).** A separate 7-agent org now reviews each paper against the real, frozen rubric of its target venue before any human submits — deliberately inverting the round-1 prior toward high flaw-recall, with a non-removable human gate and an accept that is *necessary but not sufficient* (the external venue is terminal). Every verdict logs a prediction of the real outcome, calibrating the pipeline against actual peer review over time.

The system, the C1 program, and System B are tracked in the [project writeup](/projects/worldclass-research-org).
